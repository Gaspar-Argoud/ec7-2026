1. Addressee: a visitor standing in front of the stand who speaks after a clear wake cue (button or wake phrase); bystanders, a phone call taken nearby, or two visitors talking to each other are not addressed to it and must not trigger an answer.
2. Position: a fixed stand in a busy, reverberant entrance hall under a glass ceiling, next to a coffee machine that masks the input with broadband noise for 30 s bursts — the input is near-field but never quiet and never fully predictable.
3. Channel in: one near-field microphone, same channel for all three intents (room, hours, human). Channel out - acknowledgement: a local light/tone on the box itself, generated on-device in under 1 s, so it never waits on a network round trip.
4. Channel out - answer: room-location and closing-hours answers are spoken back within a few seconds (low stakes, retryable). A "call a human" request is never answered by speech alone - it also fires a silent staff-side signal (pager/radio), because a visitor who is misunderstood must still reach a person.
5. Constraint (latency, hard number): any design whose acknowledgement can exceed 1 s under load is forbidden - that is exactly the overlap-of-voices failure the case names.
6. Constraint (approval delay, hard number): any design whose critical path needs a newly IT-approved outbound service is forbidden for go-live, because approval takes about a month and I assume (case is silent) the robot must be live within two weeks of installation.
7. Constraint (escalation, hard failure): any design that can silently fail to reach a human on request is forbidden, because that failure - not a wrong room - is what gets the robot switched off.
8. Everything else (exact wording, voice, screen, room-numbering scheme) is left open here and belongs to the integration project in session 2.
